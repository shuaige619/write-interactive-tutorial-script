Use this reference for scripts longer than 60 seconds, multi-stage tutorials, or requests for more conversational variation.

## Observed mechanisms

### Result or conflict before explanation

Openings usually establish a useful outcome, a surprising capability, a large testing cost, or a viewer pain before explaining the tool. This creates a reason to listen before definitions appear.

Apply by choosing one strong premise. Do not combine surprise, fear, savings, and authority in the same opening.

### Evidence-backed promise

Credibility comes from visible tests, concrete output, or a clearly scoped promise. The script then tells the viewer what will be selected, compared, generated, or learned.

Apply by naming observable evidence rather than broad authority claims.

### Friendly participation

The presenter repeatedly treats the viewer as someone standing beside the screen. Interaction appears as short questions, attention directions, anticipated objections, and invitations to compare personal use cases.

Apply interaction only when the screen or argument gives the viewer something to inspect.

### Action followed by payoff

Tool tutorials rarely stop at a click. They immediately show what changed, interpret whether the result is good enough, then use that result to set up the next operation.

Use this micro-sequence:

`intent → operation → visible response → judgment → next reason`

### Controlled informality

Short interjections and colloquial judgments reset attention, especially after dense explanation. They do not replace the explanation.

Use at most one informal reset in a short paragraph. Remove it if the sentence works equally well without it.

### Ownership through story and utility

When presenting a self-built tool, the creator states that it was built by the team, explains the user problem or origin, demonstrates it, and discusses tradeoffs. The tool is not framed as an unrelated third-party recommendation.

Apply by disclosing ownership early enough to preserve trust, but only after the viewer understands the problem or sees the result.

### Ending with participation

Endings commonly ask for a concrete use case, comparison, or desired feature. The call to action grows from the content rather than interrupting it.

Apply with one primary action. Avoid stacking follow, like, save, share, comment, and try into one sentence.

## Interaction functions and original examples

These are functions, not mandatory wording. Rewrite them for the topic.

### Invite attention

- “先看右边这个状态，它决定后面能不能继续。”
- “这里别只看按钮，我们看生成以后多了什么。”
- “来，对比一下处理前后的字幕节奏。”

### Anticipate doubt

- “你可能会问，既然最后要成片，为什么现在只做分镜？”
- “如果你没有真人母片怎么办？这里还有另一条路径。”
- “这个选项看起来省事，但它会不会额外计费？”

### Confirm completion

- “时间轴已经出现，说明脚本和素材检查通过了。”
- “试听结果没问题，这条声音就可以作为主音轨。”
- “看到状态变成待审核，这一阶段才算真正结束。”

### Pause before risk

- “先别点最终渲染，我们还有一个更省时间的检查。”
- “这里注意，试听也可能调用外部服务。”
- “别把参考图理解成自动复刻，它现在只是风格依据。”

### Pull forward

- “内容已经定了，下一步要解决的是谁来讲、用什么声音讲。”
- “人物和声音都齐了，现在才有条件安排镜头节奏。”
- “前二十秒通过以后，再跑完整视频才更划算。”

### Invite participation

- “你可以换成自己的文案，看看分镜会怎样变化。”
- “如果是你，你会选真人母片还是同步口型的数字人？”
- “评论区告诉我，你最想先自动化哪一个环节。”

## Variation rules

- Do not begin every section with “好”.
- Do not announce every numbered step twice.
- Alternate between attention, question, confirmation, caution, and anticipation.
- Reuse a phrase only when it becomes an intentional series signature.
- Keep the series signature to one short line; do not turn the full script into a template.
- Replace generic transitions with a state change visible on screen.

## Series continuity

For an overview episode:

- Show the final result first.
- Name the major stages without teaching every click.
- End by identifying the first obstacle the next episode will solve.

For a focused episode:

- Recall the previous result in one sentence.
- Complete one stage that produces a visible artifact or status.
- Introduce only the next dependency, not the entire remaining roadmap.

For the final episode:

- Reconnect the intermediate artifacts to the final output.
- State costs and QA limits.
- Invite the viewer to repeat the workflow with their own material.
